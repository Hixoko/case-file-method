# Hypotheses — Patient A

Status as of **M63**. Candidates carry a status, never a verdict.

---

## H1 — B12 deficiency, or copper deficiency (its near-identical twin)

**Status:** `LEADING` · `AWAITING TEST` — **and the test is compromised**

Both damage spinal cord and peripheral nerve together. Both are treatable. Both are
routinely missed.

### Supporting — ten findings, none decisive alone

| # | Finding | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Predominantly vegetarian diet | A leading dietary cause of B12 deficiency |
| 2 | Hypothyroidism | Autoimmune thyroid disease and pernicious anaemia occur in the same patients |
| 3 | Brisk arm reflexes **with** absent ankle jerks | The textbook reflex pattern of cord and nerve affected together |
| 4 | Posterior column dysfunction on SSEP | The first part of the cord this attacks |
| 5 | Upper motor neurone pattern on EMG | The second part, in the same classic pair |
| 6 | Burning and numbness below both knees | Classic early symptom |
| 7 | Structurally normal cord on MRI | Expected — chemical rather than mechanical damage |
| 8 | Urinary retention and constipation | Both described in advanced cases |
| 9 | Slow decline punctuated by sudden steps | Matches the usual course |
| 10 | Age mid-50s | Absorption falls with age, independently of diet |

### Against

- **Asymmetric weakness, left worse than right.** Deficiency states are usually
  symmetric. The strongest single argument against H1, and a point for H2.
- Joint position sense tested as *preserved* — though see
  [`contradictions.md`](contradictions.md) §2.

### What settles it

Blood tests — **but only if drawn before the methylcobalamin supplement.** Serum
B12 alone is insufficient: add **methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, active B12**,
and for copper, **copper + ceruloplasmin + zinc**. Plus intrinsic factor and
parietal cell antibodies.

### Caveat

After five years some damage may be permanent. Treating a deficiency now would be
expected to **halt the decline and recover some function** — not to undo
everything. Given the trajectory, that is still worth a great deal.

## H2 — Cervical myelopathy (cord compressed in the neck)

**Status:** `OPEN` · `AWAITING TEST` — the right test is ordered

**Supporting:** cervical spondylosis noted on the M60 screen; brisk upper-limb
reflexes point at the neck; worsening fine finger control; asymmetric weakness fits
a structural cause.

**Against:** the M60 study was a screen and did not report significant cervical cord
compression; does not by itself explain absent ankle jerks.

**What settles it:** dedicated cervico-dorsal MRI — ordered at M63. Often surgically
treatable.

---

## H3 — Spinal dural arteriovenous fistula

**Status:** `OPEN` · `AT RISK OF BEING MISSED`

An abnormal artery-to-vein connection on the surface of the cord, congesting it.
**Curable with a single procedure.**

**Supporting:** fits years of decline punctuated by sudden steps better than
anything else; fits early severe bladder failure; explains a cord that malfunctions
while looking normal on a non-contrast screen.

**Against:** no specific imaging evidence — because the imaging that would show it
has not been done.

**What settles it:** the same MRI — **but only with contrast.** The order does not
specify it. One phone call.

## H4 — Small-fibre neuropathy from the M0 chemotherapy

**Status:** `LIKELY, PARTIAL` — explains some symptoms, not the main problem

**Supporting:** burning in a stocking distribution with a **normal** nerve
conduction study is the classic picture, because NCS tests large fibres only.
Chemotherapy is among the commonest causes.

**Scope:** probably explains the burning feet. Does **not** explain the weakness,
the bladder failure, or the cord findings.

**Correction on record:** an earlier family write-up stated the M62 study ruled out
chemotherapy nerve damage. That was too broad, and is corrected here. *(See
[technique 6](../../techniques/06-contradiction-pass.md) — run the contradiction
pass over your own conclusions too.)*

## Excluded

| Candidate | Evidence that excluded it |
|---|---|
| Cancer recurrence in the spine | No marrow-replacing lesion, normal bone signal throughout |
| Cord compression | No compression, no significant narrowing, no collapsed vertebra |
| Structural damage from the M60 fall | Imaged two days later — no fracture, no displaced disc, no bleed |
| Cauda equina syndrome | Cauda equina roots unremarkable, with retention already present |
| Motor neurone disease | No denervation on needle EMG, no fasciculations; after five years it would be unmistakable |
| Myopathy | No myopathy on EMG — so not steroid or radiation muscle damage |
| Radiation plexopathy | Leaves a dramatic needle-EMG signature. Hers was clean |
| Radiation myelopathy | The region closest to the pelvic radiation is reported normal |

> **On radiation.** The M62 referral abstract framed the study as *"post-radiation
> therapy"* before any testing. Nothing in the imaging supports radiation
> myelopathy. Radiation is the easy answer that closes investigations — the
> productive question is *"what in the imaging supports radiation as the cause?"*,
> not an argument against it.
