# Questions for the next appointment — Patient A

## Before anything else — two actions, not questions

1. **Get the bloods drawn before the first dose of the methylcobalamin supplement.**
   If already started: do not stop it. Note the exact start date, tell the doctor
   and the laboratory, and ask to add methylmalonic acid, homocysteine and active B12.
2. **Call the imaging centre and confirm the scan includes contrast.**

## The questions, in priority order

**1.** *"Her arm reflexes are brisk and her ankle jerks are absent. Does that
combination point to a myeloneuropathy — and should we be testing properly for B12
and copper deficiency?"*
→ The whole case in one sentence, in language the specialty engages with immediately.

**2.** *"She started a methylcobalamin supplement on the day the B12 test was
ordered. Will that make the result unreliable — and should we add methylmalonic acid
and homocysteine?"*
→ A question about test reliability, not a correction. Most clinicians see it at
once and fix it.

**3.** *"Is the MRI being done with contrast? We'd like a dural AV fistula
specifically excluded."*
→ Naming the condition makes it far likelier the right sequences are run.

**4.** *"Her vibration sense wasn't recorded. Could you check it with a tuning fork?
The evoked potentials said the posterior columns are affected but position sense
tested normal."*
→ One minute at the bedside, resolves a real contradiction.

**5.** *"Her H-reflex was normal on both sides a month ago, but the ankle jerks were
absent at this visit. Has she genuinely lost that reflex in four weeks?"*
→ A precise question built from her own records. If yes, it is evidence of rapid
progression.

**6.** *"She has had a catheter for three months and the retention has never been
evaluated. Can we arrange a kidney ultrasound and a urology referral?"*
→ Quotes the clinician's own note back, neutrally. Unanswerable, and carries no
accusation.

**7.** *"Could the duloxetine be worsening the urinary retention, and could the
nerve-pain tablet be adding to her dizziness and falls?"*
→ Both are recognised effects. Asking lets the clinician adjust rather than defend.

**8.** *"She went from a walker to a wheelchair in four weeks. What is our plan if she
keeps declining at this rate?"*
→ The most important question on the page, and the hardest to ask. It requests a
plan, not a promise — which is what gets an actual answer.

## Also worth raising

- Can the blood panel be extended on the same draw — copper, ceruloplasmin, zinc,
  folate, vitamin E, blood count with MCV, HbA1c, intrinsic factor antibodies?
- A lying-and-standing blood pressure. Free, five minutes, and the one symptom in
  the file that nothing explains.
- A bone density scan — never done, and she is now immobile.
- Please write the **allergy** on the prescription. The field is currently blank.
- Confirm the exact composition of both supplements from the physical packs with the
  pharmacist — specifically vitamin B6 content, since high-dose B6 over long periods
  damages nerves, in a patient being treated for nerve damage for three months.

## Emergency — hospital, not an appointment

- Sudden worsening of weakness over hours or days
- New numbness in the saddle area, between the legs or inner thighs
- New loss of bowel control
- Fever, shivering, or foul or bloody urine — with a long-term catheter, infection
  can turn serious fast
- A calf that becomes swollen, warm or painful
- New back pain, especially worse at night

**And state the drug allergy aloud at every one of those visits.**
